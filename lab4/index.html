Each point represents one month. Its vertical position is the mean model-estimated sentiment score and its size represents mean favorites.

Thresholds are binned into the following: <−0.2: Negative; (−0.2,0.2): Neutral; >0.2 Positive.

Analysis:

I used the realdonaldtrump.csv dataset, which contains 43,352 tweets posted by account @realdonaldtrump on Twitter between 2009 and 2020. Duplicate records were removed (found none in data), date fields were parsed, blank mentions and hashtags were kept as a relatively small number of tweets contain the two attributes. The visualizations shows the derived time-based fields of estimated sentiment with the RoBERTa sentiment model. The model's output is treated as a model-generated estimated of the tweet sentiments (and may deviate from the ground truth as the corresponding labels are not available). This chart shows the monthly average sentiment on vertical axis, while the circle size shows mean favorites. This makes it easy to compare changes in estimated sentiment over time with monthly aggregation. Overall, the tweets show a zig-zag, yet generally stable sentiment of neutral and neutral-positive across the years.

As certain events occur, the account's publicity siginificantly improves in 2016, as shown by the increasing size of circles representing Mean Favorites. It can also be shown that since 2017, the flucutations of average sentiment reduces compared to previous years, resting mostly among values near 0. Furthermore, it is worth noting that for tweets before 2012, the monthly total tweets were significantly less. Therefore, statistical conclusions should be more carefully drawn for those time periods.
