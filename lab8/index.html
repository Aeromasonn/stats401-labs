1. Bulletin and Corpus Description

Bulletin of Duke Kunshan University Undergraduate Instruction (2021-2022). Source: official bulletin PDF; accessed September 23, 2026.

One paragraph or short policy/course block is one passage. The preparation script removes the table of contents, page numbers, short fragments, and duplicate text while preserving chapter, section, subsection, page, and passage text. The PDF produced 4,550 raw passage blocks; cleaning retained 1,335 unique passages across 183 formal sections, with an average length of 68.0 words.

3. Semantic Analysis Method

The preparation script embeds cleaned passages with all-MiniLM-L6-v2, projects the normalized vectors with UMAP (n_neighbors=15, min_dist=0.15, cosine distance, seed 401), and clusters the original embeddings with eight-cluster K-means (seed 401). Nearest neighbors use cosine similarity in the original embedding space, not 2D distance.

Representative passages and characteristic TF-IDF terms support these topic names:

6. Semantic Findings

Answer at least four questions using specific passages or patterns visible above.

  1. What are the major semantic topics?

    In fact, all topics appear multiple times. Therefore, the 'major' is in terms of the relative frequency. TF-IDF terms and representative passages support eight major topics: Culture, Society, and the Humanities; Language, Communication, and Learning; DKU Degrees and Credit Transfer; Computing, Mathematics, and Data; China and Asian History; Policy, Economics, and Global Affairs; Natural and Molecular Sciences; and Academic Rules, Grading, and Progress. The largest clusters are Academic Rules, Grading, and Progress (252 passages), Culture, Society, and the Humanities (218), and Policy, Economics, and Global Affairs (190).

  2. Which topics appear across multiple formal sections?

    Every topic crosses formal section boundaries. This makes the response towards the analysis also based on relative frequency. Academic Rules, Grading, and Progress is the most widespread, appearing in 72 of 183 sections, followed by Language, Communication, and Learning in 60, DKU Degrees and Credit Transfer in 55, and Policy, Economics, and Global Affairs in 48. For example, the degree-and-credit topic occurs in Office of Global Education and Study Away (13 passages), Degree Requirements (12), Entrance Credit and Placement (10), and Transfer of Work Taken Elsewhere (10).

  3. Which formal sections contain the most semantically diverse content?

    The maximum observed diversity from the resulting data is 5/8 topics. Six sections reach this level: Social Science, Philosophy, Political Science, History, Media and Arts, and Biology. Social Science is the most balanced of these, distributing its 13 passages across culture (2), language and learning (2), computing and data (3), China and Asian history (1), and policy and global affairs (5). History is larger but less balanced: 40 of its 73 passages concern China and Asian history and 26 concern culture and the humanities. Thus even it satisfies the Requirements to a certain degree, it is excluded from the selection.

  4. Which passages from different sections are semantically similar?

    Passage p0757 in Economics (page 262) and p1011 in Mathematics (page 325) give the same mathematical-finance course description and have cosine similarity 1.000. Passage p0898 in History (page 298) and p1046 in Media and Arts (page 333) describe the same Chinese art survey and have similarity 0.999. These pairs show that cross-listed or repeated course content can occupy different formal sections while remaining almost identical in semantic space. Nevertheless, as semantic analysis is conducted on a pretrained model, the actual precise semantic similarity would require experiment conducted on a fine-tuned task specific language model.

7. Design Description and Findings (200-300 words)

In this lab each paragraph or short policy block in the 2021-2022 DKU bulletin is treated as one passage. After removing page numbers, table-of-contents artifacts, short fragments, and duplicate text, the processed file contained 1,335 passages from 183 formal sections. Each passage was encoded with the all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence-transformer model and normalized. The original embeddings is clustered with eight-cluster K-means (seed 401 for STATS401). The topic names were based on characteristic TF-IDF terms and representative passages from each cluster. UMAP projected the embeddings into two dimensions using 15 neighbors, a minimum distance of 0.15 and cosine distance.

In the semantic map, position represents embedding similarity, color represents topic, and point size represents passage length. Search, section and topic filters, zoom, passage details, and five nearest neighbors support focused exploration. The matrix places formal sections in rows and topics in columns, with darker cells indicating more passages. Selecting a matrix cell filters the map, and selecting a point identifies its corresponding matrix cell, connecting formal and semantic structure.

The analysis found that academic rules, language and learning, and degree and credit content occur across the widest range of formal sections. Social Science, Philosophy, Political Science, History, Media and Arts, and Biology are the most semantically diverse sections, each containing five topics. Nearest-neighbor pairs also reveal repeated or cross-listed course descriptions across departmental sections, including nearly identical mathematical-finance passages in Economics and Mathematics.
