Lab 9: Geospatial Visualization

1. Dataset Description

The provided dataset contains the 50 largest economies by 2025 nominal GDP, measured in billions of current U.S. dollars. It is based on the IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2025, and values may include IMF staff estimates. The GDP table is joined to Natural Earth country boundaries using ISO-3 identifiers. Countries outside the top 50 are treated as missing data, not as zero GDP.

Data: provided GDP CSV · Boundaries: Natural Earth · Cartogram library: cartogram-chart

2. 2025 GDP Choropleth

Select a country to zoom in and highlight it in both views. Scroll to zoom further and drag to pan.

Country shape and position preserve geography; color represents GDP. A logarithmic color scale makes differences among smaller top-50 economies visible despite the strongly skewed values. Light gray indicates no data in the provided top-50 table.

3. 2025 GDP Cartogram

Area represents 2025 GDP. Select a country to zoom in, scroll to zoom further, and drag to pan; highlighting remains linked with the choropleth.

Area: 2025 nominal GDP for top-50 economies Top-50 GDP value No data; minimum context area

The cartogram intentionally distorts country shapes and positions so that larger economies use more area. Countries outside the top 50 remain neutral gray and receive only a small constant display area so they are not incorrectly encoded as zero GDP.

4. Design and Comparison

  1. Which countries are most visually prominent in each map?

    The United States and China are most visually prominent in both visualizations. They have the darkest colors in the choropleth and occupy the largest areas in the cartogram, corresponding to their substantially higher nominal GDP values.

  2. Which countries become much larger or smaller in the cartogram?

    Japan, South Korea, and many European countries appear larger than their actual territorial scale because they have relatively small land areas but large GDP volumes. Many African countries become much smaller. This cartogram includes all 50 economies in the provided dataset, not only the first 22; however, countries outside the top 50 have no provided GDP value and receive only a small context area. Africa is therefore particularly compressed and represented incompletely, rather than as having zero GDP.

  3. What geographic information does the choropleth preserve?

    The choropleth preserves the original country shapes, territorial scale, adjacency, and geographic layout. Viewers can recognize where each country is and what it looks like on a conventional world map.

  4. What geographic information does the cartogram distort?

    The cartogram distorts the shapes and territorial scales of countries. Their relative positions also shift somewhat, although the broad geographic and continental layout is largely maintained.

  5. What types of questions or tasks can the choropleth best support?

    The choropleth best supports general GDP ranking while preserving location recognition. It helps viewers identify which countries have larger GDPs without losing their actual geographic positions and territorial forms.

  6. What types of questions or tasks can the cartogram best support?

    The cartogram best supports intuitive, conceptual comparisons of economic volume. It emphasizes differences in GDP magnitude and helps viewers understand how major economies are distributed and composed across continents.

  7. How does changing from color encoding to area encoding change your perception of global economic size?

    The choropleth more clearly shows where nations are and what they look like. The cartogram makes the relative size of major economies more immediate, shifting attention from geographic territory toward how the largest GDP holders are composed across continents.
