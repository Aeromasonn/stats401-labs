Visualization Design Description

1. Why these visual channels: Both visual channels show how geographic locations differ while keeping their hierarchical relationships visible. The node-link tree is especially helpful because it shows how geodemographic information coalesces from cities and regions into countries and continents. The corresponding zoomable treemap presents the same belonging, or set, relationships from a top-down perspective. Rectangle area represents population or GDP, so larger values look larger, while color adds a second encoding channel for continent or GDP status. Together, these choices show both membership and quantitative differences rather than hierarchy alone.

2. How segmentation affects appearance and readability: Squarify emphasizes comparative relationships by producing compact rectangles, making differences in GDP area and labels easier to read. Slice-Dice uses more regular, directional subdivisions, so the geographic hierarchy is easier to follow across levels, but smaller countries can become narrow and harder to identify. In both maps, status color shows whether GDP increased, remained unchanged, or decreased, while tooltips provide exact values when labels do not fit.
